EAP Platform Design Brief
Student AI Self-Study Centre
Version: Planning Brief for Cursor / Codex
Purpose: This document describes the future design direction for the student-side AI self-study system in the EAP Platform. It should be treated as a planning and reference document only. Do not implement directly without a separate approved implementation plan.

============================================================
1. Overall Positioning
============================================================

The EAP Platform should have two major future highlights:

1. Teacher-side highlight:
   AI-assisted Teacher Live Teaching Page with AI-generated HTML teaching pages, direct file presentation, game-based classroom interaction, and teacher-student live interaction.

2. Student-side highlight:
   AI-supported Self-Study Centre for vocabulary, reading, listening, speaking, and writing.

The Student AI Self-Study Centre is different from the current calendar task system.

Current calendar system:
- Teacher creates tasks.
- Student views tasks on the calendar.
- Student submits homework or completes learning activities.
- Teacher reviews and gives feedback.

Future AI Self-Study Centre:
- Student takes a placement test.
- AI diagnoses the student's level.
- AI assigns a personalised learning path.
- Student studies independently in vocabulary, reading, listening, speaking, and writing.
- AI generates materials, practice tasks, feedback, review plans, and suitable learning games.
- The system records progress and recommends next steps.

Suggested English names:
- AI Self-Study Centre
- Student AI Learning Centre
- Personalised EAP Study Centre
- EAP AI Learning Path

============================================================
2. Relationship with Existing Platform Features
============================================================

The self-study system should not replace the current teacher-student calendar workflow.

It should work alongside the existing system:

1. Teacher-led learning:
   - Calendar tasks
   - Homework submissions
   - Teacher feedback
   - Classroom interaction
   - Teacher Live Teaching Page

2. Student-led learning:
   - Placement test
   - Personalised AI study path
   - Individual skill modules
   - AI-generated practice
   - Game-based self-study
   - Progress tracking

The two systems can share student accounts, class information, learning records, uploaded materials, AI-generated activities, game templates, and progress data.

Example: A teacher uses a vocabulary game in class. After class, the student self-study system recommends a related Memory Card Game or Matching Race activity for individual review.

============================================================
3. Entry Flow: AI Placement Test
============================================================

Before students enter the AI Self-Study Centre, they should complete an AI placement test.

Recommended duration: around 20 minutes.

Purpose:
- Diagnose the student's current EAP / IELTS-aligned English level.
- Place the student into a suitable learning path.
- Provide a skill profile across vocabulary, reading, listening, speaking, and writing.
- Recommend suitable self-study modules and daily tasks.

The system should classify students into three main levels:

1. Beginner
   - IELTS-aligned range: 5.0-6.0
   - Focus: foundation building, core academic vocabulary, basic academic reading, basic listening, sentence-level writing, short speaking responses.

2. Intermediate
   - IELTS-aligned range: 6.0-7.0
   - Focus: academic performance improvement, longer reading/listening tasks, paragraph writing, summary writing, discussion skills, collocation and academic word use.

3. Advanced
   - IELTS-aligned range: 7.0+
   - Focus: advanced academic language, critical reading, academic synthesis, seminar discussion, presentation skills, advanced writing, IELTS 7.5+ readiness.

Important note: The system should be described as "IELTS-aligned EAP self-study," not only an IELTS training system. The platform's main identity is still EAP learning in a university context.

Advanced level should support students who need IELTS 7.5+ or equivalent academic English performance for competitive postgraduate applications.

============================================================
4. Suggested Placement Test Structure
============================================================

A 20-minute test cannot fully assess all language skills, so it should be a mini diagnostic test.

Recommended first-stage structure:

Part 1: Vocabulary and Academic Word Knowledge
- Duration: around 5 minutes
- Question types: word meaning, collocation, word family, academic phrase use, synonym/paraphrase recognition.

Part 2: Reading
- Duration: around 6 minutes
- Question types: main idea, supporting details, inference, vocabulary in context, paragraph function, argument structure.

Part 3: Listening
- Duration: around 5 minutes
- Question types: main idea, key details, lecture structure, note-taking completion, speaker purpose.

Part 4: Writing / Sentence Awareness
- Duration: around 4 minutes
- Question types: sentence correction, sentence ordering, short academic sentence production, summary sentence selection, paragraph organisation.

Optional later stage: Speaking Sample
- Duration: around 2-3 minutes.
- Students record a short answer.
- AI gives feedback on content, fluency, coherence, vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation if suitable speech APIs are available.

First MVP recommendation: Start with vocabulary, reading, listening, and writing/sentence awareness. Add speaking after the basic system is stable because speaking requires recording, speech-to-text, possible pronunciation analysis, storage, and API cost management.

============================================================
5. AI Placement Report
============================================================

After the placement test, students should receive a simple AI-generated learning profile.

Example report structure:

Overall Level: Intermediate
Estimated IELTS-Aligned Range: 6.0-7.0

Skill Profile:
- Vocabulary: Intermediate
- Reading: Intermediate
- Listening: Beginner / Intermediate
- Speaking: Not assessed yet
- Writing: Intermediate

Strengths:
- Good understanding of general academic vocabulary.
- Can identify main ideas in short academic texts.
- Can understand basic lecture structure.

Areas for Improvement:
- Needs stronger collocation awareness.
- Needs more accurate summary writing.
- Needs better listening note-taking.
- Needs more advanced academic sentence patterns.

Recommended Learning Path:
- Vocabulary: Intermediate academic vocabulary and collocations.
- Reading: inference and argument structure.
- Listening: lecture note-taking practice.
- Writing: summary writing and paragraph development.
- Speaking: academic discussion and explanation practice.

============================================================
6. Five Main Self-Study Modules
============================================================

The Student AI Self-Study Centre should contain five main modules:

1. Vocabulary
2. Reading
3. Listening
4. Speaking
5. Writing

Each module should support three levels: Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced.

Each module should include:
- AI-generated learning materials
- Manager-uploaded learning materials
- AI-organised practice tasks
- AI-generated questions
- Game-based learning activities
- Review tasks
- Feedback and recommendations
- Progress tracking

============================================================
7. Vocabulary Module
============================================================

Purpose: Help students build academic and IELTS-aligned vocabulary knowledge through structured, level-based learning.

Possible learning content:
- Academic vocabulary
- IELTS vocabulary
- Merriam-Webster Vocabulary Builder style vocabulary
- Academic Word List vocabulary
- Roots and affixes
- Word families
- Collocations
- Synonyms and antonyms
- Example sentences
- Paraphrasing use

AI functions:
- Generate daily vocabulary plans.
- Explain meaning, word family, root, prefix, suffix, and usage.
- Generate example sentences.
- Generate collocation practice.
- Generate memory strategies.
- Arrange spaced repetition.
- Generate quizzes and review tasks.
- Recommend suitable games.

Level design:
- Beginner: high-frequency academic words, simple definitions, basic example sentences, bilingual support if needed, matching and memory practice.
- Intermediate: collocations, word families, academic phrase use, paraphrase recognition, sentence-level use.
- Advanced: nuanced meaning, register, synonyms for academic writing, paraphrasing in context, topic-specific academic vocabulary, 7.5+ lexical resource development.

Suitable games:
- Vocabulary Bingo
- Matching Race
- Memory Card Game
- Word Ladder
- Board Race Game
- Spin Wheel
- Hot Seat

============================================================
8. Reading Module
============================================================

Purpose: Help students improve academic reading, IELTS-aligned reading strategies, and EAP critical reading skills.

Skills:
- Main idea
- Supporting details
- Inference
- Scanning
- Skimming
- Vocabulary in context
- Argument structure
- Author stance
- Reference words
- Academic text organisation

AI functions:
- Generate graded reading passages.
- Generate IELTS-style reading questions.
- Generate summary tasks.
- Generate vocabulary-in-context questions.
- Generate paragraph matching tasks.
- Generate true / false / not given tasks.
- Generate short-answer questions.
- Generate argument sorting tasks.

Level design:
- Beginner: short academic texts, main idea, basic detail questions, vocabulary in context.
- Intermediate: longer academic texts, inference, paragraph function, IELTS-style question types, summary completion.
- Advanced: research-style texts, critical reading, argument evaluation, author stance, synthesis, 7.5+ level academic reading practice.

Suitable games:
- Treasure Hunt
- Escape Room
- Argument Sorting
- Ranking Challenge
- Summary Mission
- Board Race Game

============================================================
9. Listening Module
============================================================

Purpose: Help students improve lecture listening, note-taking, and academic listening comprehension.

Skills:
- Listening for main ideas
- Listening for details
- Recognising lecture structure
- Note-taking
- Identifying examples and evidence
- Recognising speaker attitude and emphasis
- Listening-to-summary practice

AI functions:
- Generate graded listening scripts.
- Generate listening questions.
- Generate pre-listening prediction tasks.
- Generate note-taking tasks.
- Generate post-listening comprehension tasks.
- Generate summary writing tasks.
- Provide feedback on listening performance.

Possible API requirements:
The listening module may require external AI or model APIs depending on the final implementation.

Potential technical needs:
- Text-to-Speech API to convert AI-generated lecture scripts into audio.
- Speech-to-Text API if students respond orally or summarise by voice.
- Audio file storage for generated audio and student recordings.
- Playback controls for pause, replay, speed control, and listening attempt limits.

First-stage recommendation: Start with AI-generated listening scripts and text-based listening tasks. Add generated audio through a TTS API in a later phase. Add student audio response and speech-to-text feedback after the platform is stable.

Level design:
- Beginner: short talks, clear structure, slower speed, basic detail questions.
- Intermediate: academic mini-lectures, note-taking tasks, main ideas and supporting points, summary practice.
- Advanced: longer lectures, natural speed, inference, speaker attitude, integrated listening-reading-writing summary tasks, 7.5+ readiness.

Suitable games:
- Summary Mission
- Treasure Hunt
- Ranking Challenge
- Quiz Battle
- Board Race Game

============================================================
10. Speaking Module
============================================================

Purpose: Help students practise IELTS-aligned and EAP speaking, including academic discussion, presentation, explanation, and seminar participation.

Skills:
- Short responses
- Extended speaking
- Academic discussion
- Seminar participation
- Presentation practice
- Explaining vocabulary
- Summarising reading/listening orally
- Responding to follow-up questions

AI functions:
- Generate speaking prompts.
- Simulate IELTS examiner questions.
- Generate academic discussion prompts.
- Provide follow-up questions.
- Analyse response structure.
- Give feedback on fluency, coherence, vocabulary, grammar, and pronunciation where possible.

Possible API requirements:
- Speech-to-Text for transcribing student recordings.
- Pronunciation or speech evaluation if detailed pronunciation feedback is required.
- Text feedback model for content, organisation, vocabulary, and grammar.

First-stage recommendation: Start with student recorded or typed responses. The system stores the response, and AI gives feedback based on transcript or text. Detailed pronunciation scoring can be added later.

Level design:
- Beginner: short answers, familiar topics, basic fluency, simple explanation.
- Intermediate: IELTS Part 2 style long turn, academic opinions, examples and explanations, discussion practice.
- Advanced: seminar discussion, critical response, presentation Q&A, 7.5+ extended speaking, academic argumentation.

Suitable games:
- Hot Seat
- Debate Cards
- Spin Wheel
- Quiz Battle
- Board Race Game

============================================================
11. Writing Module
============================================================

Purpose: Help students improve academic writing, summary writing, paragraph writing, and IELTS-aligned writing skills.

Skills:
- Sentence accuracy
- Academic sentence patterns
- Topic sentences
- Paragraph development
- Summary writing
- Paraphrasing
- Cohesion and coherence
- Argument development
- Grammar accuracy
- Lexical range
- Revision

AI functions:
- Generate writing prompts.
- Generate paragraph writing practice.
- Generate summary writing tasks.
- Generate IELTS Task 1 / Task 2 style practice if needed.
- Give feedback on structure, content, vocabulary, grammar, and coherence.
- Suggest revisions.
- Generate model answers or annotated examples.
- Track improvement over time.

Level design:
- Beginner: sentence accuracy, topic sentences, short paragraphs, basic summary sentences.
- Intermediate: paragraph development, summary writing, IELTS Task 2 structure, cohesion and coherence, academic vocabulary use.
- Advanced: argument quality, synthesis, source use, paraphrasing, advanced lexical and grammatical range, 7.5+ writing improvement.

Suitable games:
- Sentence Builder
- Argument Sorting
- Summary Mission
- Ranking Challenge
- Matching Race

12. Manager-Uploaded Self-Study Materials
============================================================

The manager side should allow authorised managers to upload self-study materials.

Possible material types:
- PDF
- Word
- PPT
- TXT
- Vocabulary books
- Reading passages
- Listening scripts
- Writing samples
- IELTS practice materials
- EAP course materials

AI should organise these materials for student self-study.

"Organise" may include:
- Extracting key learning points.
- Classifying materials by skill module.
- Classifying materials by difficulty level.
- Splitting long materials into daily learning tasks.
- Generating practice questions.
- Generating quizzes.
- Generating games.
- Generating review plans.
- Generating student-facing HTML learning pages.
- Generating learning advice.
- Linking materials to the student's level and progress.

Manager control is important because the system should not rely only on random AI-generated content. Manager-uploaded resources help maintain course quality and consistency.

============================================================
13. AI Game Selection in Self-Study
============================================================

The self-study system should reuse the 15 game templates from the Teacher Live Teaching Page, but adapt them for self-study.

The 15 game templates are:
1. Board Race Game
2. Vocabulary Bingo
3. Matching Race
4. Treasure Hunt
5. Escape Room
6. Quiz Battle
7. Word Ladder
8. Sentence Builder
9. Argument Sorting
10. Summary Mission
11. Memory Card Game
12. Hot Seat
13. Debate Cards
14. Ranking Challenge
15. Spin Wheel

In the teacher classroom system:
- Games are often group-based and live.
- Teacher controls the activity.
- Results are shown on the teacher dashboard.

In the student self-study system:
- Games are mainly individual practice.
- AI recommends games based on the student's skill, level, and learning goals.
- The student completes games independently.
- Progress is saved to the personal learning record.

AI should choose suitable games based on skill module, student level, learning content type, learning objective, task duration, individual/group mode, and classroom/self-study mode.

Example mapping:
- Vocabulary + Beginner: Matching Race / Memory Card Game
- Vocabulary + Intermediate: Vocabulary Bingo / Word Ladder
- Vocabulary + Advanced: Hot Seat / Debate Cards / Board Race Game
- Reading + Intermediate: Treasure Hunt / Argument Sorting
- Writing + Advanced: Summary Mission / Argument Sorting / Sentence Builder
- Speaking + Advanced: Debate Cards / Spin Wheel / Hot Seat
- Listening + Intermediate: Summary Mission / Ranking Challenge / Quiz Battle

============================================================
14. Daily AI Study Plan
============================================================

The AI Self-Study Centre should not only show modules. It should also provide a daily recommended plan.

Possible daily plan:

Today’s AI Study Plan:
1. Vocabulary: 10 academic words, 1 collocation task, 1 review game.
2. Reading: 1 short academic passage, 5 questions, 1 summary task.
3. Listening: 1 mini lecture, note-taking task, 3 comprehension questions.
4. Speaking: 1 speaking prompt, record or type answer, AI feedback.
5. Writing: 1 paragraph or summary writing task, AI feedback and revision suggestions.
6. Review: spaced repetition vocabulary review and recommended game.

The daily plan should adapt to placement level, student progress, weak skills, completed tasks, teacher/manager priorities, and upcoming class tasks.

============================================================
15. Progress Tracking
============================================================

The self-study system should track learning progress.

Possible metrics:
- Placement level
- Current skill level per module
- Completed tasks
- Accuracy rate
- Vocabulary mastered
- Reading comprehension progress
- Listening task completion
- Speaking practice count
- Writing feedback history
- Game scores
- Review streak
- AI recommendations
- Progress over time

Student dashboard could show:
- Today’s plan
- Weekly progress
- Skill radar chart
- Strengths and weaknesses
- Recommended next task
- Recent AI feedback

Teacher/manager dashboard could later show:
- Aggregated class self-study participation
- Common weak areas
- Most completed modules
- Student progress summaries
- Materials that need improvement

Privacy note: Detailed individual self-study data should be controlled carefully. The system should decide what teachers/managers can view and what remains private to the student.

18. Final Summary
============================================================

The Student AI Self-Study Centre is a major future feature of the EAP Platform.

It should provide personalised, IELTS-aligned EAP self-study for university students from Year 1 to Year 4. Before entering the self-study system, students complete an approximately 20-minute placement test. AI analyses the results and places students into Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced levels, corresponding roughly to IELTS 5.0-6.0, 6.0-7.0, and 7.0+ respectively. Advanced learning should also support students aiming for 7.5+ for competitive postgraduate applications.

The self-study system includes five modules: Vocabulary, Reading, Listening, Speaking, and Writing. Each module is level-based and can include AI-generated learning materials, manager-uploaded resources, practice tasks, games, feedback, and progress tracking. Manager-uploaded materials can be organised by AI into student-facing learning pages, daily tasks, quizzes, and suitable game-based activities.

The 15 game templates from the Teacher Live Teaching Page can also be reused in the self-study system, but adapted for individual learning rather than live classroom competition. AI can recommend suitable games based on the student's level, skill module, learning content, and learning objective.

The system should eventually provide a daily AI study plan, adaptive recommendations, progress tracking, and feedback. However, development should be phased carefully. The first safe MVP should focus on a self-study skeleton, placement test mock, and vocabulary module MVP before adding listening, speaking, advanced AI feedback, or external APIs.
